# Creative Playbook

Creative drives the majority of Meta performance and *is* the de facto targeting: the delivery system finds people who respond to what the ad says. Diverse concepts = broader retrieval reach; near-duplicate ads cluster and compete for the same slots.

## Concept development: buyer situations first
Before writing any ad, list **15–25 concrete buyer situations** from sales calls, support tickets, reviews, and forums — moments like "the founder realizes manual invoicing ate another weekend," not personas. Each situation = one creative angle. Maintain the list; coverage gaps are the test roadmap (and the opportunity-spotting skill mines them).

## Concept types (aim for ≥3 distinct types live at once)
Problem/Solution · Before/After · Founder or customer face-to-camera (UGC-style) · Data/stat lead · Testimonial · Case study · Comparison ("vs. the old way") · Pattern-break/humor. Distinct means a different *argument*, not a different background color.

## Hook hierarchy (iterate in this order)
1. **Hook** — first 3 seconds / first line. Most viewers decide here; hook rate (3s plays ÷ impressions) target 25%+, under 15% = the ad never gets a chance. Five hook shapes that work: provocative question · named specific pain · counterintuitive claim · social-proof lead · number-first.
2. **Visual treatment** — human faces outperform; 4:5 vertical as default aspect; readable without sound (captions always).
3. **Format** — static (fastest to test, shortest lifespan ~2–4 wks), video (3–5 wks), carousel, UGC-style (longest ~4–6 wks).
4. **Body copy / CTA** — last; it matters least until the hook works.

## Copy formula (long-form primary text)
DIRECT OFFER → PAIN (named, specific) → SOLUTION → PAIN EXPLAINED (the cost of not solving) → SOLUTION EXPLAINED (mechanism) → SOCIAL PROOF (number + name) → CTA. Sell the click, not the whole product. Urgency through specificity ("fix X in 60 days"), never fake countdowns.

## ICP filtering (creative as mosquito repellent)
Name the audience and the disqualifier in the creative: the role, the vertical, the situation, the price reality. Wasted impressions on non-ICP are cheaper than wasted sales calls — write copy that makes the wrong people scroll past.

## Anti-slop writing rules
No "unlock/unleash/elevate/revolutionize/game-changing/seamless/supercharge." No rhetorical-question triads. No "In today's fast-paced world." No em-dash chains stapling three clauses together. Write like a sharp operator texting a peer: concrete nouns, numbers, one idea per sentence. If a line could appear in any competitor's ad, cut it.

## Production system
- **Image-first validation**: prove a new concept as a cheap static before producing video (skip only for inherently-video concepts like demos/UGC).
- **50/30/20 production ratio** (of creatives *produced*, not budget): 50% iterations on top performers · 30% iterations on other performers · 20% brand-new concepts. Budget allocation is the optimizer's job (CBO); production allocation is yours.
- Iterations = keep the winning angle, change execution (new hook, new format, new visual). New concepts win ~10% of the time, iterations ~25% — plan test volume accordingly (winners needed ÷ 0.17 ≈ tests required).
- Every scaling ad should have its next iteration **already produced** before fatigue hits (frequency 3.0 warning = build now).

## Brief format (hand-off ready)
Per concept: buyer situation · angle/argument · hook copy written out verbatim (3 variants) · format + aspect · visual direction (reference, not vibes) · primary text draft · headline (≤5 words) · CTA button · landing page · what would make us kill it.
